VIDEO BRIEF (what the finished video must show)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Create a realistic installation video for the OptiNor height-adjustable
sound-reducing floor system. The video should show a carpenter, in a normal
indoor construction setting, performing the following installation sequence
in order:

  1. The carpenter installs the sound-reducing screw into the substrate.
     The substrate the screw is fastened into is CONCRETE.
  2. The carpenter makes height/level adjustments to the screw (turning it
     to raise or lower it so the steel rail sits level and at the correct
     height).
  3. The carpenter lays insulation between the steel rails/joists.
  4. The carpenter lays parquet flooring on top, finishing the installation.

The tone should be practical and professional — a clean, well-lit
construction/renovation environment, close-up shots of hands working with
the screw, the adjustment motion, the insulation being placed, and the
parquet boards being laid down, followed by a wider shot of the finished
floor. The video should communicate precision, ease of installation, and a
solid, professional result.

=== FROM THE PRODUCT PRESENTATION ===

OptiNor — Sound-reducing Floor System

Professional background:
Since 1999, the inventor has represented Swedish factories in Norway in the
field of adjustable sound-reducing floor systems, selling over 500,000 m²,
including the Norwegian government's VIP hotel in Inkognitogata, Oslo — the
inventor's highest-prestige project. Until 2017, projects were carried out
across all of Norway, alongside courses and presentations for contractors,
architects, acousticians and builders, both governmental and private.

From 2017 onward, development focused on new products. After a long period
of development and testing (including a pandemic in between), the current
product was finalized, developed in cooperation with the Technical Manager
at Norgips Norway AS, with quality manufacturers in Sweden producing the
various parts of the system.

What is behind the product:
  - The system meets the current C-requirements in Norway and Sweden by a
    good margin
  - The system is optimized for installation
  - The system solves the entry-height challenges of different floors
  - The system complies with environmental concerns and requirements

Benefits of installation:
  - Solves the need for centre-to-centre (CC) distance regulation in a
    simple way, resolving various assembly challenges
  - Designed for double compressive load per m²
  - The system's sound-reducing screws allow the joist to be self-standing
    during assembly — no need to cut excess screw length
  - The combined benefits result in faster installation and increased
    profit

Facts:
  - The best height-adjustable sound-reducing floor system on the market
  - Test winner based on the SINTEF report
  - Best in installation — flexible, high load capacity, quick installation
  - Adjustable CC distance — the only one on the market
  - Adapted for double sound screws, giving greater load capacity
  - No screw/bolt fastening required, resulting in no sound transmission

Additional info:
The joist of the system is the same as in Norgips' ceiling system, which
results in additional sales for Norgips on projects. Ongoing development
includes a screw extender with total height up to 50 cm, and a low-voltage
heating base for parquet flooring with cold-bridge stops.

— Dag J. Søndbø, Inventor / License holder, OptiNor AS

=== FROM THE SINTEF TEST REPORT (2023:00775) ===

Report: Laboratory measurement of impact-sound and airborne-sound
improvement for a system floor with parquet flooring.
Author: Gjermund Holøyen, SINTEF Community, Buildings and Infrastructure.
Date: 2023-08-25. Client: Optinor AS. Test method: NS-EN ISO 10140-1, -2,
-3 (accredited), rated per NS-EN ISO 717-1 and 717-2. Test location:
SINTEF Community's acoustics laboratory, Børrestuveien 3, Oslo.

Summary of measured results:
  Test object   DLw     CI,D     DRw,heavy   D(Rw+C)heavy   D(Rw+C50-5000)heavy
  OptiNor       27 dB   -12 dB   6 dB        5 dB           5 dB

Description of the test specimen:
The test specimen is a height-adjustable sound-damping floor system
consisting of a steel U-profile, 59 mm wide and 27 mm high, a sound-
reducing screw (Ø 26 mm) with external threads and a damping pad at the
base, plus a screw holder for one or two sound screws. The damping pad is
made of ethylene-propylene rubber (EPDM), 21 mm in diameter and 15 mm high.
The screw and screw holder are made of PA6+30GF plastic.

During testing, one sound screw was used per screw holder, and the
distance between the reference deck (concrete) and the top of the steel
rails was approximately 11 cm. Between the rails, 5 cm of Glava mineral
wool insulation was filled in, and 5 cm boards of Hunton Nativo wood-fibre
insulation were placed along the sides, protruding slightly above the
rails. On top of the rails, 22 mm chipboard/particleboard flooring was
laid, level with the top of the wood-fibre boards. Between the
particleboard and the parquet, an approximately 2 mm thick foamed-PE
parquet underlay was placed, and the joint between the parquet and the
surrounding frame of the standard deck was sealed with tape.

Conclusion:
SINTEF Community, on behalf of Optinor AS, measured impact-sound and
airborne-sound improvement in the laboratory for the OptiNor system floor.
Measurements were performed in accordance with NS-EN ISO 10140-1, -2 and
-3. Single-number values were calculated in accordance with NS-EN ISO
717-1 and -2. Measured impact-sound improvement was 27 dB, and measured
airborne-sound improvement was 6 dB.
